\begin{abstract}
Sequential policies with variable legal-action sets can omit which observed
item a legal action selects even when positional and other action attributes
remain available.  We study such an identity-information defect in a partially
observable stochastic card-game simulator.  The historical encoder represented
ordinary PLAY options without binding each option to the observed hand
card/template ID (the source-card type ID) that it selected.  The model therefore
saw pooled hand card IDs and an
option's hand index, but not their relational binding.  We repaired that binding
and fine-tuned four output-side modules of a frozen baseline on recent,
winner-only replay-derived decisions.  A KL term used frozen A2 weights evaluated
under the cell's encoder.  We compared the resulting policy, \EXP, with
the frozen control, \Ctl, in a common-random-number experiment prospectively
specified in a locally committed protocol before result access, over seven
fixed opponent policies and both actual turn orders.  The equal-weight
paired win-probability difference was \PrimaryEffectPP\ \pp\ (95\% stratified
paired-bootstrap interval [\PrimaryCILowPP, \PrimaryCIHighPP]; \PrimaryPairs\
pairs).  \PrimaryInference\ The secondary fixed-group point estimates were
\GrimEffectPP\ \pp\ for the
defined matched-policy family and \OtherEffectPP\ \pp\ for the other three
opponents.  In a separate episode-clustered reanalysis of
retained, outcome-selected replay units, \EXP\ approval among binary candidate--control
disagreements was \ReplayApproval\ (95\% interval [\ReplayApprovalCILow,
\ReplayApprovalCIHigh]); \ReplayApprovalInference\ A four-cell analysis that
embeds this primary contrast and adds two mechanistic cells constrains
mechanistic attribution; two retained null experiments bound broader claims.
The study separates representation validity, imitation agreement, paired
gameplay performance, and opponent-group heterogeneity; it does not claim
universal improvement or superhuman play.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Imperfect-information games are useful environments for studying sequential
decision making because an agent must act from a partial view of a stochastic
process while anticipating another policy.  Poker systems such as DeepStack and
Pluribus illustrate the scientific difficulty of this class
\cite{moravcik2017deepstack,brown2019pluribus}; they are context, not empirical
comparators for the card-game agent studied here.  Our question is narrower:
what happens when a concrete legal-action representation defect is repaired,
and do offline imitation metrics rank the repaired policy in the same way as
paired simulated outcomes?

The distinction matters because a representation can be more faithful to the
available observation without increasing agreement with outcome-selected,
held-out recorded actions.  Conversely, a policy can improve terminal outcomes against a
defined opponent set without reproducing more historical actions.  Sequential
imitation learning is exposed to distribution shift because a learned policy
changes the states it visits \cite{ross2011dagger}.  The present study does not
implement DAgger and claims none of its guarantees; that work motivates
separating held-out action agreement from on-policy outcomes because
learner-induced state distributions can differ.

Legal-action interfaces add a separate representational hazard.  Invalid-action
masking restricts selection to valid actions \cite{huang2022invalidmasking};
that legality mechanism is separate from whether features encode each valid
option's semantic attributes.  Permutation-invariant and
permutation-equivariant set architectures provide a vocabulary for variable-size
collections \cite{zaheer2017deepsets}.  Here the defect was
bounded: an ordinary PLAY option carried an index into the observed hand, but
the v2 feature path left its source-card-ID field at zero.  The policy retained
pooled state tokens for hand card IDs, the normalized hand index, and other
option attributes, but lacked the mapping between index and identity.  Omission
of that relation does not by itself imply that two simultaneous option vectors
were identical.

We contribute (i) a corpus-level audit of this identity omission; (ii) an identity-aware
repair coupled to a restricted, A2-anchored output-module update; (iii) a
fresh, paired, order-balanced gameplay confirmation; (iv) a matched four-cell
representation-by-training ablation; and (v) an explicit comparison with
episode-clustered recorded-action agreement and retained null experiments.
Reporting was informed by recommendations to quantify aggregate uncertainty and
improve reproducibility through transparent reporting and artifact sharing
\cite{agarwal2021statistical,pineau2021reproducibility}.  Our hash-based
provenance scheme is a study-specific implementation.
The opponents are a frozen engineering population rather than a probability
sample.  Public competition rating and rank are not outcomes in this paper.

\section{Formal problem setting}

We model the simulator as a finite-horizon partially observable stochastic
game.  At engine step $t$, the acting agent receives observation $o_t$ and a
variable legal-action collection
$A_t=\{a_{t1},\ldots,a_{tK_t}\}$.  The observation contains only information
exposed by the competition interface.  A policy scores each legal action,
\begin{equation}
  s_\theta(o_t,a_{tj})=h_\theta\!\left(g_\theta(o_t),
  \phi_\theta(o_t,a_{tj})\right),
\end{equation}
and selects from $A_t$ subject to deterministic runtime constraints.  Here
$g_\theta$ represents global observation features and $\phi_\theta$ represents
one legal option.  Because $K_t$ varies, option identity is part of the policy
contract rather than merely an output label.

For a fixed observation, define an exact blind-input collision when distinct
legal actions $a\neq a'$ have the same complete option-head input after the
source-card-ID coordinate is set to zero.  A weaker, component-level omission
occurs whenever that coordinate is zero although the selected observed card has
a known type ID.  The defect examined here is the latter: ordinary PLAY
options refer to entries of the observed hand, the historical encoder assigned
source ID zero, and the repair binds the in-range selected card ID.  This ID is
not a unique physical-instance serial.  Other
features, including normalized hand index, can still distinguish two options.
The v2 state embedding pools visible hand-identity tokens, while the local
option vector carries the hand index; neither encodes which identity occupies
that index.  We therefore describe the defect as a missing option--item binding
and measure exact within-state collisions separately rather than assuming them
from the missing coordinate.

For paired evaluation, let $W^{(1)}_{ci}$ and $W^{(0)}_{ci}$ be \EXP\ and
\Ctl\ win indicators for paired unit $i$ in opponent-by-order cell $c$.
Losses and draws are zero for the primary endpoint.  Define
\begin{equation}
 D_{ci}=W^{(1)}_{ci}-W^{(0)}_{ci}, \qquad
 \widehat\Delta=\frac{1}{14}\sum_{c=1}^{14}
       \frac{1}{n_c}\sum_{i=1}^{n_c}D_{ci}.
 \label{eq:estimand}
\end{equation}
Every $n_c$ equals 200, so $\widehat\Delta$ also equals the mean over all
2,800 paired units after complete execution.  The estimand is finite-population:
it concerns the seven frozen opponents, two actual-order conditions, policy
packages, and engine build named in the protocol.

The replay estimand is different.  At an outcome-selected recorded decision
where \EXP\ and \Ctl\ select semantically different actions, the recorded action may
approve \EXP, approve \Ctl, or approve neither.  Approval is
\begin{equation}
  \widehat A=\frac{N_{\EXP}}{N_{\EXP}+N_{\Ctl}},
\end{equation}
with neither-approved cases reported as abstentions.  Episodes, not decisions,
are the uncertainty clusters because within-episode decisions are dependent
\cite{field2007clustered}.  This conditional quantity is not gameplay value.

\section{Representation defect and identity-aware intervention}

The historical v2 branch resolved ordinary PLAY options with no explicit board
area to no source object.  The feature serializer then emitted
\texttt{source\_card=0}.  The repair is deliberately narrow: with its package
flag enabled, an in-range legal-option index resolves
\texttt{hand[option.index]} and serializes that observed item's card/template
ID (the source-card type ID).  It
does not inspect hidden state.  A parity audit over 4,659 decisions and 24,065
options found 2,853 changed PLAY source values across 658 decisions, all in the
main-action context, and no other feature changes.

The retained corpus contains \CorpusDecisions\ decisions and
\OrdinaryPlayOptions\ ordinary PLAY options.  Reconstructing the blind encoder
left \UnresolvedPlayOptions\ of those options unresolved; the repaired rows
bound all of them.  The retained hand-position coordinate matched the raw index
for all \IndexMatchedPlayOptions\ instances, and all
\UniqueIndexMultiPlayStates\ states with multiple PLAY options had distinct
indices.  Of \PlayStates\ states containing PLAY, \MultiIdentityStates\
(\MultiIdentityStatePct\%) exposed at least two distinct PLAY card IDs.
An exact within-state audit of the complete blind option-head input found
\ExactCollisionGroups\ cross-identity collision groups in
\ExactCollisionStates\ states (\ExactCollisionInstancePct\% of PLAY-option
instances).  Thus the retained rows verify pervasive identity-coordinate
omission, not exact full-vector aliasing.  All affected options occurred in the
main-action context.  Source-frequency outputs use anonymous tokens so no
proprietary card names or identifiers enter the release.  Because the exact
collision subgroup contained no rows, its output-disagreement and recorded-action
approval rates are not estimable; the machine-readable audit records zero counts
and null rates explicitly, while the noncollision group equals the full corpus.

\section{Replay data and restricted output-module training}

The retained feature corpus contains 47,653 decisions: 38,254 training rows,
3,361 internal-validation rows, and 6,038 rows from six held-out teams.  It
spans 472 episode identifiers and 69 team labels.  Every retained row is labeled
with positive reward and the historical \texttt{daily\_top\_episode} source, so
the corpus is outcome-selected rather than representative of play; demonstrator
expertise and recorded-action optimality were not independently established.  The split
manifest gives held-out-team precedence and records no duplicate keys.  A
team is ``held out'' only from this refresh's gradients, internal validation,
and checkpoint selection; A2's original training corpus is unavailable, so the
teams are not proven unseen to the initialized model.  A
nominal temporal holdout is invalid: its eight files are empty stubs and supply
no rows.  All retained rows have a null observation field, and the original
Aug. 14--15 replay observations used to mine the corpus were deleted by the
historical pipeline.  Thus the retained feature rows---not end-to-end replay
extraction or re-resolution of \texttt{hand[option.index]}---are the reproducible
training source.  The separate parity audit tests the binding on observations.
The accompanying data card documents intended use and privacy boundaries in the
spirit of dataset documentation practice \cite{gebru2021datasheets}.

Training initialized student and teacher from A2, froze all parameters outside
the option projection plus score, count, and value heads, and optimized the
student on CPU for three epochs with AdamW.  The frozen A2-weight teacher was
evaluated under the same encoder as its cell; for the identity-aware cell this
is not identical to blind \Ctl\ behavior.  The learning rate was $10^{-4}$, weight decay
$10^{-5}$, batch size 256, seed 20260816, gradient-norm limit 1.0, and KL
distillation weight 0.5.  Eight of 19 exported arrays changed, exactly those of
the four trainable modules; frozen arrays were byte-preserved.

The configured fresh-data weight was 0.999.  Historical batch rounding made the
implied rehearsal target zero: each epoch processed 38,254 fresh and zero
rehearsal rows.  We therefore use \emph{conservative} only for frozen parameter
scope, baseline initialization, and KL anchoring.  We do not describe the
realized update as a 99.9/0.1 mixture or claim a formal safe-improvement
guarantee.  Baseline-anchored policy improvement provides related motivation,
not a theorem for this update \cite{laroche2019spibb}.

\section{Evaluation protocol and statistical analysis}

The fresh protocol was prospectively committed in the local repository before
result access.  It freezes \Ctl, \EXP,
the seeded engine, production-engine integrity sentinel, seven opponent package
hashes, actual-first and actual-second conditions, disjoint seed ranges,
physical-seat alternation, and 200 candidate--control pairs in every cell.
Within a pair, candidate and control records share opponent, engine seed, actual
order, and physical seat.  The frozen command also specifies the opponent
environment and maximum-decision limit, but the per-game rows do not serialize
either field; those two settings therefore cannot be independently verified
row by row.  Common random numbers can reduce nuisance variation when a valid
coupling is implemented \cite{glasserman1992crn}; we verify the serialized
coupling fields in every retained pair rather than inferring them from the
design label.

The complete schedule comprises \PrimaryPairs\ paired units and \PrimaryGames\
engine games.  The binary primary outcome maps a win to one and loss or draw to
zero.  The secondary utility maps win, draw, and loss to $1,0,-1$.  An exception,
nonterminal truncation, illegal action, candidate/control/opponent policy error,
incomplete pair, hash drift, production-sentinel change, or pairing mismatch
invalidates a cell rather than being deleted or imputed.  All scheduled fresh
pairs completed with zero policy errors.  The frozen runner did not record
per-game latency; parallel wall time is not substituted.

The primary interval uses 100,000 bootstrap replicates.  Each replicate resamples
complete candidate--control pairs with replacement independently within all 14
cells, then averages the cell means.  The exact two-sided McNemar test conditions
on candidate-only plus control-only wins and tests symmetry of the discordant
direction.  This is sharper than the weak null of an equal mean across 14 fixed
strata, for which the paired bootstrap interval is the primary summary.  Fourteen individual cell tests are
secondary and Holm-adjusted together.  Opponent, order, family, and utility
summaries remain descriptive.  No meta-weighted estimate is computed because
the historical frozen weight file is absent.

The four-cell ablation matches the same engine, opponents, seeds, orders, seats,
and pair counts across cells.  It embeds the already reported C4--C1 primary
rows and adds newly executed C2 and C3 candidate cells; it is not a second
independent C4 confirmation.  The analysis reuses the \Ctl\ records for C1.  The other
cells are separately executed simulations matched by seed-condition identifier,
not shared realized stochastic worlds or trajectories.  C1 is blind encoder plus A2; C2 is identity-aware
encoder plus unchanged A2; C3 is blind encoder plus matched output-module
retraining; C4 is identity-aware encoder plus the matched update (\EXP).  C2 and C3 are
mechanistic, not deployment candidates.  Five binary contrasts are Holm-adjusted;
the difference-in-differences interaction is descriptive.  The C3 transform
zeroed exactly 37,199 source fields in 47,653 retained rows, completed all three
epochs, realized zero rehearsal rows, and exported model hash
\texttt{d22d0b52...8b70}.

The mismatch is substantively plausible.  Replay approval asks which policy

\section{Discussion}

The case separates four quantities that are often collapsed: representational
validity, held-out imitation agreement, paired gameplay value, and opponent-group
heterogeneity within the frozen battery.  Binding a legal action to the observed item it
selects repairs a factual defect.  It does not imply that inherited embeddings
are calibrated, that every downstream choice improves, or that the recorded
outcome-selected action is uniquely valuable.  The four-cell results are therefore
informative for distinguishing encoding, training, and their interaction, while
one training seed and separately fitted cells leave causal components unresolved.

The restricted update is a practical audit pattern for a competent baseline.
Freezing most parameters and retaining a KL term to frozen A2 weights under the
cell's encoder reduces the dimensions in which learning can move.  It does not provide a monotonic policy
improvement guarantee, particularly because the realized stream contained no
rehearsal rows and because replay and gameplay state distributions differ.

Paired common-random-number evaluation supports a paired comparison of two fixed
policies and can improve precision when the coupling is valid.  Pairing does not
make the result universal: opponent packages, actual order, engine build,
runtime constraints, and decks remain part of the estimand.  Similarly, a
clustered replay interval respects episode dependence but cannot turn a
conditional imitation proxy into long-horizon value.

The negative results and evidence conflicts narrow the story.  Additional
temporal or bounded sequence structure did not demonstrate benefit in their
bounded tests.  Historical favorable aggregate claims were not
used when their rows, weights, or estimator could not be reconstructed.  This
reduces apparent evidence but provides a direct, auditable path from every
included number to retained bytes.

\section{Limitations}

First, the seven opponents are a deliberately frozen convenience population,
several with related development histories.  They are neither random nor
exhaustive.  Bootstrap intervals describe seed variation under this battery and
do not support population-wide generalization.

Second, only one training seed and one outcome-selected retained corpus were
evaluated.  All corpus rows came from winning top-episode units, and the replay
reanalysis further required a prespecified team and exact target deck.  These
selection rules limit both imitation and held-out estimands; the replay intervals
are conditional on the fixed team sets and include no between-team population
uncertainty.  The C2 identity-only
cell supplies a previously zero feature to weights not trained for it; C2 and C3
are mechanistic diagnostics.  Model-only disagreement omits runtime constraints,
and multi-identity-state associations do not identify a causal subgroup effect.

Third, original replay observations for the retained Aug. 14--15 feature corpus

\section{Statistical algorithms}

For each primary bootstrap replicate $b$, let $I^{(b)}_{ci}$ be $n_c$ indices
sampled with replacement from cell $c$.  We compute
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\Delta^{(b)}=\frac{1}{14}\sum_c\frac{1}{n_c}
   \sum_{i\in I^{(b)}_c}D_{ci},
\end{equation}
and take the 2.5 and 97.5 percentiles.  McNemar's exact two-sided value is twice
the smaller Binomial$(b+c,0.5)$ tail, clipped at one, where $b$ and $c$ are the
two discordant directions.  Holm adjustment orders the 14 cell values and
enforces monotone step-down adjusted values.  Ablation bootstrap draws reuse a
matched seed-condition identifier across all four separately executed cells;
this is not a shared realized trajectory.  Replay bootstraps sample episode identifiers;
the sequence-oracle interval samples roots.  Seeds, replicate counts, and draw
handling are fixed in the protocols and summary JSON.
