\begin{abstract}
Sequential policies with variable legal-action sets can fail because distinct
legal actions are mapped to the same policy input.  We study an instance in a
partially observable stochastic card-game simulator.  The historical encoder
represented ordinary PLAY options without binding each option to the observed
hand item that it selected.  We repaired that binding and fine-tuned only four
policy heads of a frozen baseline on recent expert-replay decisions while
retaining a distillation anchor.  We compared the resulting policy, \EXP, with
the frozen control, \Ctl, in a preregistered common-random-number experiment over
seven fixed opponent policies and both actual turn orders.  The equal-weight
paired win-probability difference was \PrimaryEffectPP\ \pp\ (95\% stratified
paired-bootstrap interval [\PrimaryCILowPP, \PrimaryCIHighPP]; \PrimaryPairs\
pairs).  \PrimaryInference\ In a separate episode-clustered reanalysis of
retained replay units, \EXP\ approval among binary candidate--control
disagreements was \ReplayApproval\ (95\% interval [\ReplayApprovalCILow,
\ReplayApprovalCIHigh]); \ReplayApprovalInference\ A matched four-cell
ablation and two retained null experiments constrain mechanistic attribution.
The study separates representation validity, imitation agreement, paired
gameplay performance, and robustness across opponent groups; it does not claim
universal improvement or superhuman play.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Imperfect-information games are useful environments for studying sequential
decision making because an agent must act from a partial view of a stochastic
process while anticipating another policy.  Poker systems such as DeepStack and
Pluribus illustrate the scientific difficulty of this class
\cite{moravcik2017deepstack,brown2019pluribus}; they are context, not empirical
comparators for the card-game agent studied here.  Our question is narrower:
what happens when a concrete legal-action representation defect is repaired,
and do offline imitation metrics rank the repaired policy in the same way as
paired simulated outcomes?

The distinction matters because a representation can be more faithful to the
available observation without increasing agreement with a held-out
demonstrator.  Conversely, a policy can improve terminal outcomes against a
defined opponent set without reproducing more historical actions.  Sequential
imitation learning is exposed to distribution shift because a learned policy
changes the states it visits \cite{ross2011dagger}.  The present study does not
implement DAgger and claims none of its guarantees; that work establishes why
held-out action agreement and on-policy outcomes answer different questions.

Legal-action interfaces add a separate representational hazard.  Invalid-action
masking controls which outputs may be selected but does not ensure that two
valid outputs are distinguishable \cite{huang2022invalidmasking}.  Set and
relational models provide a general vocabulary for retaining item identity in
variable collections \cite{zaheer2017deepsets}.  Here the defect was bounded:
an ordinary PLAY option carried an index into the observed hand, but the v2
feature path left its source-identity field at zero.  Different legal plays
could therefore have identical option features where their selected item should
have distinguished them.

We contribute (i) a corpus-level audit of this aliasing; (ii) an identity-aware
repair coupled to a heads-only, baseline-anchored imitation update; (iii) a
fresh, paired, order-balanced gameplay confirmation; (iv) a matched four-cell
representation-by-training ablation; and (v) an explicit comparison with
episode-clustered expert-action agreement and retained null experiments.  The
reporting follows recommendations to quantify aggregate uncertainty and preserve
artifact provenance \cite{agarwal2021statistical,pineau2021reproducibility}.
The opponents are a frozen engineering population rather than a probability
sample.  Public competition rating and rank are not outcomes in this paper.

\section{Formal problem setting}

We model the simulator as a finite-horizon partially observable stochastic
game.  At engine step $t$, the acting agent receives observation $o_t$ and a
variable legal-action collection
$A_t=\{a_{t1},\ldots,a_{tK_t}\}$.  The observation contains only information
exposed by the competition interface.  A policy scores each legal action,
\begin{equation}
  s_\theta(o_t,a_{tj})=h_\theta\!\left(g_\theta(o_t),
  \phi_\theta(o_t,a_{tj})\right),
\end{equation}
and selects from $A_t$ subject to deterministic runtime constraints.  Here
$g_\theta$ represents global observation features and $\phi_\theta$ represents
one legal option.  Because $K_t$ varies, option identity is part of the policy
contract rather than merely an output label.

For a fixed observation, an action-identity collision occurs when distinct
legal actions $a\neq a'$ have the same blind option signature on the fields used
to distinguish them.  The defect examined here concerns ordinary PLAY options
whose legal-option indices refer to different entries of the observed hand.
The historical encoder assigned source identity zero; the repair resolves the
in-range index and binds the selected observed item.  This is a factual repair
to an observed feature, not a claim that all remaining state or action aliasing
is eliminated.

For paired evaluation, let $W^{(1)}_{ci}$ and $W^{(0)}_{ci}$ be \EXP\ and
\Ctl\ win indicators for paired unit $i$ in opponent-by-order cell $c$.
Losses and draws are zero for the primary endpoint.  Define
\begin{equation}
 D_{ci}=W^{(1)}_{ci}-W^{(0)}_{ci}, \qquad
 \widehat\Delta=\frac{1}{14}\sum_{c=1}^{14}
       \frac{1}{n_c}\sum_{i=1}^{n_c}D_{ci}.
 \label{eq:estimand}
\end{equation}
Every $n_c$ equals 200, so $\widehat\Delta$ also equals the mean over all
2,800 paired units after complete execution.  The estimand is finite-population:
it concerns the seven frozen opponents, two actual-order conditions, policy
packages, and engine build named in the protocol.

The replay estimand is different.  At a recorded decision where \EXP\ and
\Ctl\ select semantically different actions, the recorded expert action may
approve \EXP, approve \Ctl, or approve neither.  Approval is
\begin{equation}
  \widehat A=\frac{N_{\EXP}}{N_{\EXP}+N_{\Ctl}},
\end{equation}
with neither-approved cases reported as abstentions.  Episodes, not decisions,
are the uncertainty clusters because within-episode decisions are dependent
\cite{field2007clustered}.  This conditional quantity is not gameplay value.

\section{Representation defect and identity-aware intervention}

The historical v2 branch resolved ordinary PLAY options with no explicit board
area to no source object.  The feature serializer then emitted
\texttt{source\_card=0}.  The repair is deliberately narrow: with its package
flag enabled, an in-range legal-option index resolves
\texttt{hand[option.index]} and serializes that observed item's identity.  It
does not inspect hidden state.  A parity audit over 4,659 decisions and 24,065
options found 2,853 changed PLAY source values across 658 decisions, all in the
main-action context, and no other feature changes.

The retained corpus contains \CorpusDecisions\ decisions and
\OrdinaryPlayOptions\ ordinary PLAY options.  Reconstructing the blind encoder
left \UnresolvedPlayOptions\ of those options unresolved; the repaired rows
bound all of them.  Of \PlayStates\ states containing PLAY, \MultiIdentityStates\
(\MultiIdentityStatePct\%) exposed at least two distinct PLAY identities.
Seventeen exact blind-signature groups contained multiple source identities,
covering \CollisionInstancePct\% of PLAY-option instances.  All affected
options occurred in the main-action context.  Source-frequency outputs use
anonymous tokens so no proprietary card names or identifiers enter the release.

Training initialized student and teacher from A2, froze all parameters outside

guarantee.  Baseline-anchored policy improvement provides related motivation,
not a theorem for this update \cite{laroche2019spibb}.

\section{Evaluation protocol and statistical analysis}

The fresh protocol was committed before result access.  It freezes \Ctl, \EXP,
the seeded engine, production-engine integrity sentinel, seven opponent package
hashes, actual-first and actual-second conditions, disjoint seed ranges,
physical-seat alternation, and 200 candidate--control pairs in every cell.
Within a pair, candidate and control share opponent, engine seed, actual order,
physical seat, and opponent environment.  Common random numbers can reduce
nuisance variation when a valid coupling is implemented \cite{glasserman1992crn};
we verify the coupling fields in every retained pair rather than inferring it
from the design label.

The complete schedule comprises \PrimaryPairs\ paired units and \PrimaryGames\
engine games.  The binary primary outcome maps a win to one and loss or draw to
zero.  The secondary utility maps win, draw, and loss to $1,0,-1$.  An exception,
nonterminal truncation, illegal action, candidate/control/opponent policy error,
incomplete pair, hash drift, production-sentinel change, or pairing mismatch
invalidates a cell rather than being deleted or imputed.  All scheduled fresh
pairs completed with zero policy errors.  The frozen runner did not record
per-game latency; parallel wall time is not substituted.

The primary interval uses 100,000 bootstrap replicates.  Each replicate resamples
complete candidate--control pairs with replacement independently within all 14
cells, then averages the cell means.  The exact two-sided McNemar test conditions
on candidate-only plus control-only wins.  Fourteen individual cell tests are
secondary and Holm-adjusted together.  Opponent, order, family, and utility
summaries remain descriptive.  No meta-weighted estimate is computed because
the historical frozen weight file is absent.

The four-cell ablation reuses the same engine, opponents, seeds, orders, seats,
pair counts, and \Ctl\ arms.  C1 is blind encoder plus A2; C2 is identity-aware
encoder plus unchanged A2; C3 is blind encoder plus matched retraining; C4 is
identity-aware encoder plus matched retraining (\EXP).  C2 and C3 are
mechanistic, not deployment candidates.  Five binary contrasts are Holm-adjusted;
the difference-in-differences interaction is descriptive.  The C3 transform
zeroed exactly 37,199 source fields in 47,653 retained rows, completed all three
epochs, realized zero rehearsal rows, and exported model hash
\texttt{d22d0b52...8b70}.

The mismatch is substantively expected.  Replay approval asks which policy
reproduces one historical action in states generated by historical agents.  It
does not reward a different action for downstream value.  Paired gameplay
instead compares terminal outcomes on trajectories induced by each policy.
The estimands differ in state distribution, conditioning, horizon, and analysis
unit.  Their disagreement demonstrates metric dependence under distribution
shift; it does not establish that expert data are poor or that simulated
gameplay is unbiased.

\section{Negative and null experiments}

reaggregation (Table~\ref{tab:negative}).  An actual-second two-turn temporal

selection screen was not pooled with confirmation, and the result does not show

two semantic deviations.  Its effect was \SequenceEffectPP\ \pp\ with
root-cluster interval [\SequenceCILowPP, \SequenceCIHighPP], far below its
prespecified +3-point gate.  This constrains one proposal-and-confirmation
procedure; it is not a test of planning as a class.

\section{Discussion}

The case separates four quantities that are often collapsed: representational
validity, held-out imitation agreement, paired gameplay value, and robustness
across opponent populations.  Binding a legal action to the observed item it
selects repairs a factual defect.  It does not imply that inherited embeddings
are calibrated, that every downstream choice improves, or that the recorded
expert action is uniquely valuable.  The four-cell results are therefore
essential to distinguish encoding, training, and their interaction.

The restricted update is a practical audit pattern for a competent baseline.
Freezing most parameters and retaining a baseline-distillation term reduces the
dimensions in which learning can move.  It does not provide a monotonic policy
improvement guarantee, particularly because the realized stream contained no
rehearsal rows and because replay and gameplay state distributions differ.

Paired common-random-number evaluation improves the comparison of two fixed
policies when seeds and exogenous conditions can be coupled.  Pairing does not
make the result universal: opponent packages, actual order, engine build,
runtime constraints, and decks remain part of the estimand.  Similarly, a
clustered replay interval respects episode dependence but cannot turn a
conditional imitation proxy into long-horizon value.

The negative results and evidence conflicts narrow the story.  Additional
temporal or bounded sequence structure did not automatically produce detectable
benefit in their frozen tests.  Historical favorable aggregate claims were not
used when their rows, weights, or estimator could not be reconstructed.  This
reduces apparent evidence but provides a direct, auditable path from every
included number to retained bytes.

\section{Limitations}

First, the seven opponents are a deliberately frozen convenience population,
several with related development histories.  They are neither random nor
exhaustive.  Bootstrap intervals describe seed variation under this battery and
do not support population-wide generalization.

Second, only one training seed and one retained corpus were evaluated.  The C2
identity-only cell supplies a previously zero feature to weights not trained for
it; C2 and C3 are mechanistic diagnostics.  Head-only disagreement omits runtime
constraints, and collision-state associations do not identify a causal
subgroup effect.

Third, original replay observations for the retained Aug. 14--15 feature corpus
and an original environment lock are missing.  The nominal temporal holdout is
empty.  Training can be repeated from retained feature rows, but feature mining
cannot be reconstructed from source replays.  The fresh runner did not store
per-game latency.

Fourth, the third-party engine, source, binaries, card data, decks, private
replays, and opponent implementations cannot be redistributed under the
available terms and privacy constraints.  The release reproduces statistics,
tables, and figures from processed outcomes, not engine trajectories.  Rights
review is also required before redistributing policy weights or placing a
permissive license on the companion package.

Fifth, historical seven-policy and meta-weighted aggregates lack their raw wave
files and frozen weight table.  Historical CERT-B prose calls 529 disagreements
``decisive,'' although its reconstructed binary denominator is 398, and its
episode rows are absent.  These values are recorded as conflicts or unsupported
and omitted from the main evidence.

Finally, author identity, affiliations, corresponding-author ORCID, CRediT
roles, funding, conflicts, acknowledgments, complete historical AI-tool use,
archive DOI, and release ownership are not established by repository evidence.
They require human confirmation before submission.

\section{Conclusion}

Distinct observed card plays were legal yet not identity-distinguishable to the
historical option encoder.  We repaired that binding, applied a restricted
baseline-anchored update, and evaluated the result with paired simulation and
episode-clustered replay diagnostics.  \PrimaryInference\ The offline approval
interval did not establish a corresponding improvement in expert-action
agreement.  The broader methodological recommendation is to audit legal-action
identity explicitly, match representation and training ablations, pair
stochastic evaluations at their natural unit, and preserve negative results and
provenance conflicts alongside favorable findings.

\section{Statistical algorithms}

For each primary bootstrap replicate $b$, let $I^{(b)}_{ci}$ be $n_c$ indices
sampled with replacement from cell $c$.  We compute
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\Delta^{(b)}=\frac{1}{14}\sum_c\frac{1}{n_c}
   \sum_{i\in I^{(b)}_c}D_{ci},
\end{equation}
and take the 2.5 and 97.5 percentiles.  McNemar's exact two-sided value is twice
the smaller Binomial$(b+c,0.5)$ tail, clipped at one, where $b$ and $c$ are the
two discordant directions.  Holm adjustment orders the 14 cell values and
enforces monotone step-down adjusted values.  Ablation bootstrap draws reuse a
paired world across all four cells.  Replay bootstraps sample episode identifiers;
the sequence-oracle interval samples roots.  Seeds, replicate counts, and draw
handling are fixed in the protocols and summary JSON.

\section{Evidence exclusions and reproducibility boundary}

Surviving historical files contain 5,500 nominal direct pair records, including
600 exact overlapping B0 pairs.  One proposed reconstruction of four favorable
runs has exactly 3,600 pairs but a raw-row effect of +5.472 \pp, not the reported
approximately +4.5.  The seven-policy field JSON, aggregation code, and
\texttt{meta\_weights.json} do not survive.  Historical CERT-B rows do not
survive, preventing reconstruction of its clustered interval.  These values are
recorded in the ledger but excluded from evidentiary results.

The branch requested as \texttt{grim-5k-variance-floor} existed locally only as
\texttt{archive/grim-5k-variance-floor}.  Large packages and engines are ignored
files fixed by hash rather than Git objects.  The release therefore supports
processed-result reproducibility.  Engine-level trajectory reproduction remains
conditional on lawful third-party access and cannot be guaranteed by the
authors' package.
