Dear Editors,

We seek consideration of “A Protocol for Validating Pairing Assumptions in
Seed-Matched Evaluations of Black-Box Game-Playing Agents” as an **APS Open
Science Protocol Article**.

When two game-playing agents are evaluated under the same recorded seed, the
shared value names a matched schedule — nothing more. Whether that result may
legitimately be analyzed as a paired comparison is an empirical question, and
most evaluation practice answers it by assumption. We submit a Protocol Article
that turns the assumption into an executable, fail-closed workflow: artifact
and boundary-seed identity, row-level schedule parity, identical-arm trace
projections re-executed across fresh-process, worker-count, enqueue-order, and
pool-lifecycle contexts, bounded stochastic-source auditing with explicit
outcome rules, cross-arm event-alignment checks when event identities exist,
and a machine-checked admission map that downgrades or suppresses paired
wording whenever required evidence is absent.

The protocol is validated three ways. A standalone synthetic conformance suite
reproduces seed-conversion, stateful draw-shift, timing, and process-state
failures, demonstrates event-keyed repair for a declared ontology, and rejects
schema or manifest tampering. In a restricted-engine case study, a prospectively
frozen deterministic preflight passed with zero required mismatches across
3,000 executions, while 99 of 200 fixed timed-search seed-condition clusters
disagreed on the recorded trace projection. And the reporting consequence is
real: one apparently favorable historical comparison failed a repeated-control
repeatability audit and was suppressed, while a later comparison acquired under
a narrower frozen rule was admitted even though its policy contrast — a single
fixed-battery descriptive contrast that remained unresolved — did not become
more favorable; admission tracked evidence rather than favorability.

The contribution boundary is deliberate. Common-random-number theory, structured
random streams, paired-seed statistics, counter-based and event-keyed
randomness, rollout preservation (Rollout Cards), trace-assurance contracts,
deterministic workflow testing (AEVAL), protocol disclosure cards, paired
noise-floor measurement, metamorphic testing, simulation verification and
validation, and A/A diagnostics are prior art; the manuscript cites and
contrasts each. The contribution is their integration into an executable
black-box evidence-to-claim decision, with chronology discipline: the frozen
case-study protocol contained the eight-stage ladder and binary gates before
acquisition, and the generic completed-case claim taxonomy is disclosed as a
post-acquisition conservative formalization that future users must freeze
before outcome collection.
