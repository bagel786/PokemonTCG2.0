## Metadata caveats

- The PMLR, NeurIPS, and JMLR primary records above do not publish article DOIs; none were added.
- The official NeurIPS BibTeX exports for *Deep Sets* and *Pointer Networks* leave the page field empty, but their official NeurIPS `Metadata.json` records supply pages 3391--3401 and 2692--2700, respectively; those primary-record ranges are used here.
- The FLAIRS primary record has volume 35 but no conventional page range in its DOI metadata; no page or article number was inferred.
- Accented and compound author names are encoded in LaTeX in `references.bib`. The DAgger and SPIBB PMLR HTML/BibTeX exports shorten several title-page names; this bibliography follows the title-page forms for St\'ephane Ross, Geoffrey J. Gordon, J. Andrew Bagnell, R\'emi Tachet des Combes, and the NeurIPS title-page form Marc G. Bellemare. BibTeX suffix syntax is used for Hal Daum\'e III because the ACM/Crossref deposit misparses the suffix.
- arXiv:2512.24145 changed title between versions. The bibliography uses the current v3 title, *When Does Pairing Seeds Reduce Variance? Evidence from a Multi-Agent Economic Simulation*, retains 2025 as the original-submission year, and records the 2026 revision in a note. Its earlier title must not be used as though it were current.
- `sharma2025pairedseeds` and `buffalo2026eventkeyed` are arXiv preprints, not peer-reviewed journal articles as of this audit. Their `10.48550/arXiv.*` identifiers are arXiv-issued DataCite DOIs, not journal-publication DOIs. Claims relying on them must be labeled accordingly.

## PEVL novelty boundary supported by this audit

The defensible novelty is **not** that this work discovered that identical seeds can be insufficient. Glasserman and Yao already condition CRN guarantees on system structure and event timing; L'Ecuyer and coauthors provide stream/substream machinery for synchronization; Sharma analyzes the precision benefit of paired seeds when correlation is favorable; and Buffalo, Pearson, and Klein directly explain event misalignment under stateful PRNGs and propose event-keyed randomness.

The proposed contribution is instead an **operational, black-box-compatible, fail-closed validation framework** for deciding which paired agent-evaluation claims may be admitted when researchers cannot replace the simulator RNG or inspect every opponent implementation. PEVL integrates artifact and seed-namespace checks, schedule parity, identical-arm controls, serial/parallel trace tests, stochastic-source auditing, event-alignment evidence where available, and a prespecified rule that suppresses a contrast when its required gate fails. Bouthillier and Patterson support the need to distinguish kinds of reproducibility and account for variation, but neither source supplies this exact claim-admission ladder.

This is a targeted primary-source audit, not a systematic-review proof of priority. The manuscript should use “we propose” or “we introduce” for the operational framework and avoid absolute first-ever claims.
