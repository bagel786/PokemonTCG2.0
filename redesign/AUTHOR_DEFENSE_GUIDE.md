# Author Defense Guide

Status: **PENDING HUMAN COMPREHENSION AND SIGN-OFF**.

## One-sentence paper claim

Different seed-matched claims require different evidence; a prospective planted-
failure comparison across two open systems found useful branch-specific
distinctions but rejected the full reference implementation as a universal
recommendation and exposed missing confirmatory outcome banks.

## Questions the author must answer without reading a script

### 1. Why is this not a cumulative ladder?

Because a statistically paired estimator can be justified by its randomized,
repeated-measures, probability-sampling, hierarchical, or joint-model design
without exact replay or event alignment. Replay and mechanistic counterfactuals
are different claims.

### 2. What do Branches A-E mean?

A: recorded schedule matching. B: justified paired inference. C: scoped
deterministic replay. D: CRN coupling and variance. E: semantic event-value
alignment.

### 3. How is ground truth known?

The harness construction fixes the intended failure and branch label before
outcomes. The label is a fact about the injected wrapper/design, not a learned
label from favorable results.

### 4. What is detection?

The fraction of construction-labeled hard-invalid cells that a method suppresses
or fails closed on. It is not sensitivity to naturally occurring failures.

### 5. What is false suppression?

The fraction of construction-valid cells that a method unnecessarily downgrades
or suppresses. It prevents “always reject” from looking like a good validator.

### 6. Why is B5 important?

B5 models or at least gates on the cluster/analysis unit. For Branch B it caught
all designed hard-invalid cells without suppressing valid cells in this bank,
while B7 also caught all but suppressed 37.5% of valid cells. This is not an
equivalence claim.

### 7. Did B7 pass its gate?

No. Overall hard-invalid detection was 89.4%; Branch-C detection was 48.75%;
pooled false suppression was 14.6%. The predeclared requirements were at least
95% detection per eligible branch and no more than 10% pooled false suppression.

### 8. Why did Branch C perform poorly?

The Ising runner did not inject the intended S4/S8 clock residual mechanics even
though the frozen labels called replay invalid. The mismatch audits both the
benchmark wrapper and the classifier. It cannot be interpreted as natural replay
failure sensitivity.

### 9. What is wrong with hold'em S3?

The event-keyed adapter used the same keyed random action rule in both arms,
ignoring random-versus-conservative policy. The paired differences are therefore
identically zero. That is a degenerate benchmark warning, not decisive CRN gain.

### 10. Are coverage, Type-I error, and power validated?

No. The promised A/A mirror/equal-temperature banks, fixed-effect constructions,
and repeated S8 outcomes were not retained. Centered bootstrap outputs are
diagnostics only.

### 11. Why not generate the missing outcomes now?

Because decision outcomes have already been inspected. A repaired acquisition
would be a new study and needs a new freeze, seed bank, and independently checked
output contract.

### 12. What does R<1 mean?

The observed paired-difference variance is smaller than variance after frozen
cross-seed re-pairing. It quantifies precision in that bank; it does not prove
valid coupling or event alignment.

### 13. What does Kendall tau-b 0.81 mean?

The eight method orderings on the predeclared M1-M2 device are positively
associated between two systems. It does not prove broad cross-domain consistency.

### 14. What costs are actually measured?

System pair runtime, stored artifact bytes, configuration-field counts, and a
50-run trace-logging microbenchmark. Per-method runtime and full per-method
bundle bytes were not retained.

### 15. Was the study registered?

No. The protocol was frozen in public git history at commit 62ad878 before final
outcomes. No OSF or comparable registration exists.

### 16. What is novel?

Potential novelty lies in evaluating claim-specific validation strategies under
planted seed/coupling failures with false-suppression and cross-domain costs. CRN,
event-keyed RNG, replay, A/A tests, and rollout records are not new.

### 17. What role does the Pokémon case play?

Motivation only: 99/200 trace-projection disagreement clusters and retrospective
210/2800 and 458/2800 record disagreements. Restricted assets, no event ontology,
and retrospective rules prohibit primary or pooled use.

### 18. Are the systems redistributable?

The study-authored wrappers/data are released under MIT. RLCard 1.2.0 and the
Ising toolkit are both MIT; verbatim installed license files are included.
Dependencies are installed, not vendored.

### 19. What is the go/no-go and why?

`NOT_READY_DO_NOT_SUBMIT`: the B7 gate failed; M4-M6 and method cost outputs are
missing; wrapper defects exist; and no independent human review or author
comprehension sign-off has occurred.

### 20. What would change the answer?

A new prospectively frozen campaign that repairs S3/S4/S8, retains all promised
statistical banks and per-method costs, passes independent output-contract tests,
and receives accountable human scientific review.
